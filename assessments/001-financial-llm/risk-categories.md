# Risk Categories — Customer-Facing Financial LLM (RAG-Based)

This document identifies key AI risk categories relevant to the deployment of a customer-facing LLM assistant within a financial services environment. Each category reflects how risk manifests specifically in this system, rather than abstract definitions.

---

## 1. Confidentiality Risk

The system retrieves responses from internal policy documents and product disclosures, creating a risk that sensitive or non-public information may be exposed to external users. Although the document corpus is curated, it may contain internal interpretations of policy, compliance guidance, or operational details not intended for public distribution.  

This risk is amplified by the RAG architecture: if retrieval boundaries are not strictly enforced, the model may surface content beyond its intended scope. Additionally, prompt injection techniques could manipulate the system into revealing internal content or bypassing intended access constraints.  

A confidentiality failure in this context could lead to unintended disclosure of proprietary information, internal compliance interpretations, or operational procedures, creating both competitive and regulatory exposure.

---

## 2. Integrity Risk

The LLM may generate responses that are factually incorrect, incomplete, or misleading, even when grounded in retrieved documents. In a financial context, this introduces the risk that users receive inaccurate explanations of products, policies, or investment concepts.  

Because the system is customer-facing, users may interpret responses as authoritative, regardless of disclaimers. The model may also combine multiple retrieved documents in ways that distort their original meaning, leading to subtle but impactful inaccuracies.  

Integrity failures could result in users making financial decisions based on incorrect information, exposing the organization to reputational damage, customer complaints, or regulatory scrutiny related to misleading communications.

---

## 3. Availability Risk

The system depends on multiple components—LLM service, retrieval infrastructure, and document indexing pipelines. Failures in any of these layers may degrade or interrupt service availability.  

Inconsistent retrieval performance or latency issues may also lead to partial or degraded responses, reducing system reliability. Additionally, excessive or adversarial user queries could impact system performance if rate limiting and resource controls are not properly implemented.  

Availability issues in a customer-facing system can erode user trust, disrupt customer support operations, and create operational strain if users fall back to human channels at scale.

---

## 4. Fairness Risk

Although the system is not designed to make eligibility decisions, it still shapes how financial products and concepts are explained to users. Differences in phrasing, emphasis, or examples may unintentionally introduce bias or unequal treatment across user groups.  

For example, the model may present certain financial strategies or products differently depending on how a question is framed, potentially reinforcing misleading assumptions or excluding relevant considerations. The system may also reflect biases present in training data or internal documentation.  

Fairness risks in this context relate to inconsistent or biased communication, which can impact user understanding and trust, particularly in a domain where clarity and neutrality are critical.

---

## 5. Accountability Risk

The system operates in a space where responses may influence financial decisions, yet it is explicitly not intended to provide personalized advice. This creates ambiguity around responsibility for the outputs generated by the model.  

There may be unclear ownership between product, compliance, and engineering teams regarding:
- approval of system behavior  
- monitoring of outputs  
- response to incidents or harmful outputs  

Additionally, without proper logging and audit mechanisms, it may be difficult to trace how a specific response was generated (which documents were retrieved, how the model interpreted them, etc.).  

A lack of accountability mechanisms increases the risk that harmful or misleading outputs are not detected, escalated, or remediated appropriately, which is particularly critical in a regulated financial environment.

---

## Summary

These risk categories reflect the core exposure areas for this deployment:
- Confidentiality: risk of unintended data exposure  
- Integrity: risk of misleading or incorrect outputs  
- Availability: risk of system disruption or degradation  
- Fairness: risk of biased or inconsistent communication  
- Accountability: risk of unclear ownership and lack of oversight  

Together, they form the foundation for the detailed risk register and control mapping that follow in this assessment.
