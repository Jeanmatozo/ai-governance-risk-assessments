# Cross-Framework Mapping  
## ISO/IEC 42001:2023 ↔ NIST AI RMF 1.0  
### Scenario: Financial LLM Deployment (Internal Advisory Assistant)

---

## Overview
This table maps selected ISO/IEC 42001 Annex A controls to corresponding NIST AI Risk Management Framework (RMF) subcategories. The goal is to show how different frameworks align in governing AI systems, while translating their requirements into plain language.

---

## Cross-Framework Alignment

| ISO 42001 Control | NIST AI RMF Subcategory | Plain Language Requirement |
|------------------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| **A.5.7 — AI System Impact Assessment** | **MAP 1.1 — Context is established and understood** | The organization must understand how the AI system is used, what decisions it influences, and what could go wrong if it fails. |
| **A.5.13 — Monitoring and Logging** | **MEASURE 2.1 — AI system performance is monitored** | The system must be continuously tracked to detect incorrect outputs, unsafe behavior, or drift over time. |
| **A.5.15 — Human Oversight** | **MANAGE 1.3 — Human-AI interaction is appropriately governed** | Humans must remain involved in reviewing and validating AI outputs, especially for high-impact decisions like financial guidance. |

---

## Key Observations

- ISO 42001 focuses on **organizational controls and accountability**, while NIST AI RMF emphasizes **risk lifecycle management**.
- There is strong alignment between:
  - **Impact assessment (ISO)** and **context mapping (NIST)**
  - **Monitoring (ISO)** and **measurement (NIST)**
  - **Human oversight (ISO)** and **risk management (NIST)**
- Both frameworks reinforce the idea that AI governance is not just technical — it is **operational and decision-focused**.

---

## Why This Matters

Mapping across frameworks allows organizations to:
- avoid duplicating compliance efforts  
- align technical testing with governance requirements  
- translate abstract standards into actionable controls  

In this scenario, the alignment highlights that managing a financial LLM requires:
- understanding its impact  
- continuously monitoring its behavior  
- ensuring human accountability remains central
